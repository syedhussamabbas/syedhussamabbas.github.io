About

I'm a Plant Molecular Biologist with 4+ years of experience in post-transcriptional gene regulation, RNA biology and plant stress responses. I'm currently working with Dr. Tibor Csorba in the Plant Stress Biology Group, Genetics and Biotechnology Institute, MATE University in Gödöllő, Hungary. My PhD work sits at the intersection of RNA surveillance (NMD pathway), alternative splicing and heat stress memory in plants.

My core research interests include:

  • Post-transcriptional and translational regulation: alternative splicing, NMD pathway and RNA surveillance
  • Heat stress transcription factors (HSFA2, HSFA3) and thermomemory
  • Co-transcriptional regulation and transcription elongation fidelity
  • Abiotic stress signalling and molecular responses in plant systems
  • Autophagy and protein quality control: selective degradation and proteostasis during stress recovery
  • Post-translational modifications (PTMs) in stress regulatory networks

My work has been published in The Plant Cell and The Plant Journal on transcription fidelity, stress responses, and gene regulation in plants, with additional first-author research work submitted to the Journal of Experimental Botany.

Beyond academia, the regulatory principles I work on (including RNA quality control, stress-responsive gene networks, isoform-level switches) translate directly into questions relevant to crop resilience, trait engineering and RNA-based biotechnology.

I'm currently seeking postdoctoral opportunities in RNA biology, plant stress responses, or gene regulation, where I can continue pushing at the interface of transcriptomics, molecular genetics and stress biology.

I believe science, at its best, is service to humankind! :)

Research
Journal article

Nonsense-mediated decay promotes heat stress recovery independently of HsfA2/HsfA3 in Arabidopsis

Journal of Experimental Botany · Submitted 2026

Heat stress triggers alternative splicing of HSFA2, the master regulator of the heat shock response, generating several mRNA isoforms whose balance shifts through acclimation and recovery. This work maps how nonsense-mediated decay governs which of those isoforms accumulate, and what that means for how a plant remembers a heat event days after it ends.

The central finding is that NMD promotes recovery from heat stress through a route independent of both HsfA2 and HsfA3, so the decay machinery does more than clear faulty transcripts. It actively helps the plant reset. In NMD-deficient backgrounds the isoform patterns depart from what current AS-NMD models predict, pointing to further layers of regulation still to be described.

Journal article

Transcription fidelity and control of alternative splicing contribute to heat stress survival in Arabidopsis

The Plant Cell · 28 October 2025 Show publication →

We explored how heat stress disrupts transcription fidelity and alternative splicing in Arabidopsis, leading to a surge in transcription errors. We also uncovered how key transcription elongation factors such as TFIIS, together with the NMD pathway, work to correct these mistakes and keep gene expression accurate.

Together these mechanisms form an RNA quality control system that plants rely on when temperatures rise, giving a clearer picture of how they stay resilient in a warming world.

Journal article

INCREASED DNA METHYLATION 3 forms a potential chromatin remodelling complex with HAIRPLUS to regulate DNA methylation and trichome development in tomato

The Plant Journal · 23 March 2025 Show publication →

Our findings establish the role of SlIDM3, a regulator of DNA methylation in tomato. Genome-edited slidm3 mutants show a hairy phenotype with increased glandular trichomes, closely resembling the hairplus (hap) mutant.

Protein purification and methylation analysis revealed that SlIDM3 forms a chromatin remodelling complex with HAP, shaping methylation patterns and controlling trichome development. These insights into epigenetic regulation open routes for crop improvement.

Book chapter

Deciphering the Plants' Response to Flood-Induced Hypoxia and Anoxia at the Molecular Level

Springer, Cham · 24 April 2025 Show publication →

This chapter examines how flooding, made more frequent by climate change, affects plant health. It focuses on the hormonal and molecular signalling that underpins flood tolerance, particularly ethylene and reactive oxygen species (ROS).

Dissecting these pathways clarifies how plants manage low-oxygen stress and points towards breeding or engineering flood-tolerant crops, which matters directly for food security in a changing climate.
