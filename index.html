About

I'm a final-year PhD candidate in the Plant Stress Biology Group at MATE University (Hungarian University of Agriculture and Life Sciences) in Gödöllő, Hungary, working under Dr. Tibor Csorba. My thesis, "Post-transcriptional regulation of heat stress response and thermomemory by nonsense-mediated decay in Arabidopsis thaliana," looks at how plants use a post-transcriptional quality control pathway to shape their response to heat stress and their memory of that stress once temperatures return to normal.

My broader interests cover RNA surveillance mechanisms, transcriptome plasticity under abiotic stress, and the interplay between transcriptional and post-transcriptional quality control, including how these mechanisms extend to hormone signalling components such as brassinosteroid pathway transcripts under thermal stress.

Before Hungary, I completed my MSc in Plant Breeding and Genetics at the University of Agriculture, Faisalabad, Pakistan. I'm defending my PhD by the end of 2026 and am currently looking for a postdoctoral position starting around March 2027.

Research
Isoform I

AS-NMD and HSFA2 isoform dynamics

Heat stress triggers alternative splicing of HSFA2, the master regulator of the heat shock response, producing several mRNA isoforms. I'm mapping how nonsense-mediated decay governs which of these isoforms accumulate during thermal acclimation, and how that shapes the heat shock response over time.

Isoform II

NMD as a pro-recovery factor

My data point to NMD supporting recovery from heat stress through a pathway that runs independently of both HsfA2 and HsfA3. This suggests the decay machinery does more than clear out faulty transcripts. It actively helps the plant reset once the stress has passed.

Isoform III

Isoform accumulation in NMD-deficient plants

In NMD-deficient mutant backgrounds, I see distinct patterns of isoform accumulation that current models of AS-NMD don't fully explain, pointing to additional layers of regulation still waiting to be described.
